## friday, 13 november. butterflies on the way to mangaluru

it was around six in the morning when i left koramangala. still dark, with mangaluru ahead of me and my first bike, mayeri, carrying me into my first road trip on a bike of my own.

i was going for devfest. i had also given myself a few more days to ride up the coast, and i wanted mayeri to see all of it.

the mountains arrived and suddenly there were curves, climbs and descents to work through. i was new to this, but i had joshi bhaiya's voice notes in my head, explaining how to approach exactly these things.

i kept remembering his advice as the road changed. when a section went smoothly, i was a little impressed with myself, then straight back to paying attention to the next one.

and then shiradi ghat had butterflies everywhere.

there were so many along that stretch that i felt like i'd ridden into a disney film. between them, the greenery and the sound of water flowing, i kept having these moments of, oh man, i am actually here, doing this.

[shiradi is the ghat stretch on nh 75 between bengaluru and mangaluru](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2144606&lang=2&reg=48). for me, that name now comes with butterflies and the sound of water.

somewhere along the way i stopped for roadside idli that cost twenty rupees. idli and chutney became my dependable answer whenever i wasn't sure what to eat.

ordering could take a bit of work when we didn't share a language. i would try english or hindi, sometimes use gestures, and hope we had understood each other well enough.

the ride also had moments that brought all my attention back to the road. i stopped with other people to help at minor accidents, including one where an innova slipped on a patch of road, its wheels locked, and it turned around by roughly half a circle.

we helped, and it turned out okay. seeing it happen made me more alert to the surface and to what other people might need room to do.

i was enjoying the ride, and i wanted to ride responsibly around everyone else using that road. the more i noticed, the more deliberately i rode, and the more comfortable i became with mayeri.

i made it to mangaluru that day, without an overnight break on the way. i found lodging about two minutes' walk from wrkwrk, where the event would be held the next morning.

in the evening i went to panambur beach. it was the first time in my life i had seen a beach.

[panambur sits near mangaluru's harbour on the arabian sea](https://dk.nic.in/en/places-of-interest/). i had ridden through the ghats that morning, and now i was watching the sun go down by the water.

i'd already loved a sunset in mussoorie. this was another one entirely, and i couldn't get over how different the same part of the day could feel in a different place.

back at the lodging, i ordered some local food and rested. i was already thinking mangaluru could easily take five days of its own; i had only just arrived and there was so much i wanted more time for.

## saturday, 14 november. devfest, then back to the beach

i could walk to [devfest mangaluru at wrkwrk triangle](https://gdg.community.dev/events/details/google-gdg-cloud-mangalore-presents-devfest-mangaluru-2026/). after the previous day's ride, spending most of saturday at the event also gave me a break from the bike.

i met good people and listened to talks that made the journey there feel worthwhile. there were speakers and community leaders i was glad to meet, and conversations i wanted to continue beyond the event.

i kept thinking i should spend more time giving talks in cities like mangaluru. i would like to meet more of the people building outside the biggest tech hubs, hear what they are working on, and have useful technical conversations with them.

by the evening i went back to the beach, this time with a friend i'd met at devfest. i told her that i'd only seen a beach for the first time the day before.

we had snacks, spent time by the water and watched the sunset. she was happy to be there, and i was happy to have someone to share this very new thing with.

two evenings by the sea, and i still wanted to keep looking. then it was back to the lodging, with an early start for gokarna ahead.

## sunday, 15 november. the sea on my left

i filled the tank early and left mangaluru around six, heading north through udupi toward gokarna. there were stretches where the sea was beside the road on my left, with big stones along the edge and the breeze coming across.

riding alongside it felt surreal. i'd spent the last two evenings getting used to seeing the sea from a beach, and now it was keeping me company on the road.

the route took us through villages and coconut plantations. much of the riding was wonderfully smooth, with long stretches of very little traffic, though construction diversions and rough patches still needed attention.

i wanted mayeri to see all of this, the villages and coconut plantations as well as the coast.

i visited prasanna anjaneya temple near kundapura, then continued north to murudeshwar temple. these were stops along the coastal ride before reaching gokarna.

[murudeshwar's temple complex stands on kanduka hill, with the arabian sea around three sides](https://karnatakatourism.org/en/destinations/murudeshwara/). there is a huge shiva statue and a tall gopura, the temple's entrance tower, in that setting by the sea.

reaching gokarna took a different kind of attention. the turns got sharper, and there were steep climbs and descents on the approach to the beaches.

i'd been looking forward to a beach town and had to remember that the beaches here sit among hills. joshi bhaiya's voice notes were back in my head as i worked through the turns and got there safely.

i stayed at the hosteller gokarna, a beautiful place where i met a few backpackers. there were a lot of birds in gokarna too, another thing i kept noticing while i was there.

that evening i went to kudle beach to record video messages for my friends. the dogs on the beach joined in, having fun around me while i tried to make the videos, so some of them ended up in the recordings too.

and then there was the sunset.

the sky kept changing colour after the sun had gone down. i kept watching how far the colour spread, and how the view carried on changing when i might otherwise have thought the sunset was finished.

it was my last evening by the sea on this trip. the changing sky felt like gokarna was saying goodbye before i'd even left.

## monday, 16 november. inland through jog falls

before leaving gokarna, i visited mahabaleshwar temple. [the temple is part of the town's long history as a pilgrimage destination](https://karnatakatourism.org/en/destinations/gokarna/), alongside the beaches that had brought me out with my camera the evening before.

then i rode inland through the honnavar and jog falls corridor. after following the coast north, i was turning back toward the interior of karnataka.

i stopped at the jog falls viewpoint, and honestly, it was disappointing that day. [the sharavathi forms four named cascades here](https://shimoga.nic.in/en/places-of-interest/), but from where i was standing there was so little visible flow that it barely looked like a waterfall.

the falls were far away, with none of the spray reaching the viewpoint. it was hard to feel much connection to the water from that distance, especially with so little of it coming down.

and it was so hot when i reached. i'd enjoyed the air through most of the karnataka ride, but standing there in my riding jacket felt unbearable.

afterwards i continued through sagara toward shivamogga.

i wasn't going to complete the whole return to bengaluru in one go. shivamogga became the overnight stop, and i chose sri durga lodge after reading reviews that had made me curious about the place.

i wanted to try this stay rather than book a fancy hotel. it had small rooms in a much bigger complex, and the whole place seemed to run manually.

what impressed me was how well it was managed. the parking and the rooms were clean, and everything felt organised despite the scale of the place.

it was a good place to stop for the night with mayeri. bengaluru could wait until the next morning.

## tuesday, 17 november. fog, four protein bars and a friendly stop

i left shivamogga early, around six again. it was foggy and so cold.

my pinlock visor insert earned its place that morning. the [anti-fog insert inside the visor](https://pinlock.com/technology) kept it from fogging up in the cold air, something i appreciated very directly while riding through the morning.

i had carried protein bars through the whole trip without opening them. on this final stretch to bengaluru, i finally opened four of them.

eventually i found a restaurant run by a couple and stopped there because it looked friendly. it was quite a big place, but the people running it made it feel approachable.

after that stop, i carried on to bengaluru and reached koramangala on 17 november.
