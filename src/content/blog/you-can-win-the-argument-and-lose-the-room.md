some people leave every conversation having won it. and slowly, quietly, they run out of people to talk to.

i've been collecting the habits that do this. not from a distance. i've caught myself in at least half of them this year.

so read this the way i wrote it. not as a list to diagnose your friends with. as a mirror.

## how you argue

**needing to be right in every conversation.** movies, relationships, politics, the correct way to cut mangoes. the conversation drags for hours until the other person finally hands over an exhausted "okay, you're right."

here's the thing about that win. you got the sentence. they got the lesson. and the lesson was: talking to you is expensive.

**treating every piece of criticism as an attack.** not everyone is out to eat you. some people mean well, and it shows if you let it.

take feedback with a grain of salt, sure. use your discretion. then adapt or adopt what's useful and drop the rest, without drawing a sword first.

**confusing cruelty with honesty.** you don't have to be harsh to be direct. "i'm just being honest" is doing a lot of cover work for "i didn't bother being kind."

most of the time it isn't a personality trait. it's just that nobody taught us the gentler way to say true things. the good news is it's learnable.

**refusing to apologize.** you can just be wrong sometimes. it costs surprisingly little.

accountability reads as maturity from a mile away. the word sorry, said early and meant, buys back rooms that hours of being right cannot.

## how you treat other people's lives

**dismissing what you haven't experienced.** someone tells you what happened to them, and because it never happened to you, it must be exaggerated.

the world is much bigger than your frame of reference. someone else's experience doesn't need your co-signature to be real.

**telling people how to be.** how to dress. how to be more feminine, more manly, more agreeable, more marriageable. unsolicited edits to someone's identity.

nobody asked. and it was never yours to edit.

**belittling other people's wins.** someone else winning takes nothing from you. there is no scoreboard where their promotion subtracts from your life.

the constant need to shrink someone's good news says a lot more about our own insecurities than about their win.

**mocking people who tried and failed.** at least they tried. the loudest laughter always comes from the stands, from people who have never once been on the field.

if it didn't work, they learned something. if you mocked it, so did everyone watching you.

## how you center yourself

**making every conversation about you.** they share a thing. you have a bigger thing. they open up about a hard week, and two sentences later everyone is somehow discussing your year.

the world doesn't revolve around you. the sun does not rise and set on your schedule. a conversation is a place you share, not a stage you book.

**making one person your whole personality.** obsessing over someone is not a red flag by itself. we've all been there. the trouble starts when the obsession replaces your judgment.

there's a line i heard once that stuck with me: at some point, start doubting the benefit of the man instead of forever giving him the benefit of the doubt. it applies in every direction.

## the test i use

none of this is about becoming agreeable. you can be direct, confident, and full of opinions, and still be someone people feel bigger around instead of smaller.

the check is simple. after talking to you, do people feel met, or do they feel beaten?

win fewer arguments. keep more rooms.
