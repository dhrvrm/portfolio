i was doing the thing i do. confessing a pattern to s, feeling appropriately terrible about it, announcing that i want to be better.

i expected comfort. this is what she said instead.

> you already know the solution. you just don't want it badly enough, because it isn't really costing you anything yet.
>
> the guilt you're feeling now will fade soon too. that's why you feel like you want to be better right now. wanting something in the moment and wanting it innately are different things.
>
> that's what women mean by "i want you to want to do the dishes." i want you to want to help me. care for me, so that you'd want to.
>
> not do it because you feel guilty. that's just the guilt talking. you don't actually care, you just don't like feeling this way.

four sentences and my week rearranged itself.

## guilt has a half-life

look at what she actually diagnosed. my "i want to change" wasn't a decision. it was a symptom.

guilt spikes at the moment of the fight, the missed call, the forgotten thing. and while it's spiked, the wanting feels enormous and real. i will be different. starting now.

but guilt decays. it always decays. and the wanting that was riding on it decays at exactly the same rate. by next tuesday, when nobody is upset with me, the enormous wanting is a mild memory.

care doesn't spike. it just runs, level, through ordinary weeks. it's there on days nobody is disappointed in me. that's the whole difference. guilt is about my discomfort. care is about the other person.

## the dishes were never about dishes

the line "i want you to want to do the dishes" gets mocked as irrational. s's reading of it is the clearest i've heard.

nobody wants a partner who does chores under compulsion, resentfully, keeping score. the dishes are a proxy. the actual request is: care about my days the way you care about your own, so that helping isn't a favour you perform, it's a want you have.

doing it out of guilt fails that request even when the dishes get done. because guilt-service isn't for the other person. it's for you. you're not caring for them, you're managing your own bad feeling, and people can tell. that's why guilt-driven help never quite lands as love.

## the test i took from it

so now, when i catch myself saying i want to change, i ask the question her four sentences imply.

would i still want this next tuesday, when nobody's upset with me and the guilt has gone home?

if yes, it's care. act on it, build the habit, accept the cost upfront.

if no, at least be honest about what it is. a feeling passing through, wearing the costume of a decision.

she was right about the other thing too. i did already know the solution. knowing was never the missing piece. wanting it on an ordinary tuesday was.
